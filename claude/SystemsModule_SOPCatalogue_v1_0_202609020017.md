## The leading statement

This sits at the head of the catalogue, before any item. It is chapter 24 promoted from a list entry to the frame, because it is the thesis the rest hangs off.

> Every procedure here exists for one reason: to turn something you care about into something that happens without anyone having to choose it.
>
> When you catch yourself reminding the team to care, that is not a training problem. It is a design signal. The reminder is telling you a default is missing.
>
> Tick the moments where that is true in your venue. We will write the default.

Chapter 24's own line sits under it: *turning a value into a default is a design job, not a motivational one.*

## How the catalogue works

The operator ticks two things per item: **we do not have one**, and **we have one but it is not working**. Both are diagnostic. A "not working" tick becomes a candidate breakpoint on the fragility map, so the catalogue feeds the diagnosis rather than bypassing it.

The venue's two weakest Calm Index domains float to the top, pre-suggested. Welcome and endings lead for every venue regardless.

Ticking generates a starting procedure in cue, routine, reinforcement and owner form, then the module interviews the operator to fit it to their venue. Everything generated lands as `draft`, still faces the three method questions, and still cannot reach `installed` without a rostered-off record.

**The soft ceiling.** Past four ticks the module does not refuse. It says the minimum system set is three or four, asks which three to install first, generates the rest into the library, and starts only the chosen three as live. Optional items do not count toward the ceiling.

Item 11 merges the opening set with carrying the value story: the room and the offer say the same thing at the same moment.

Item 13 is deliberately loose. It is a prompt rather than a procedure, marked optional, excluded from the ceiling count. Ticking it costs nothing and if it surfaces nothing, nothing is lost.

Items 7 and 14 are the two bookends and lead for every venue type. The book weights the peak and the end; the welcome is the other end of the same experience.

## Part B. Venue type

The nineteen do not change. Items 7 and 14, the welcome and the ending, lead for every type. Each type adds one further lead and at most two type-specific items.

| Type | Third lead | Two additions |
|---|---|---|
| Cafe | Prep and roster | Morning rush changeover; counter split, takeaway against dine-in |
| Restaurant | Handover | Sitting changeover and table turn; the no-show call |
| Bar | Margin defaults | Refusal of service as an operational default, not the legal obligation; last drinks and close-down |
| Pub | Handover | Multi-area handover, bar to bistro to gaming; security handover |
| Hotel food and beverage | Handover | Meal-period transitions; the interface with front office |
| Large format | Weekly shape | Section-lead handover; how a standard travels across a big team |

Two additions per type is the ceiling. Past that it becomes a per-type template library and the method is gone.

## Part C. Outside our remit

The module lists these headings, asks whether one exists, and where it does not, names the body that sets the standard. **It never drafts the policy.** Payroll, employment and safety carry legal exposure; that is a lawyer's or an accountant's job.

These sit outside the audit, never receive a verdict, and never count toward the minimum system set. The three method questions do not apply to a superannuation policy.

Filtered by venue type, so liquor licensing does not appear for a cafe.

| Heading | Authority | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Pay rates, penalties and overtime | Fair Work Ombudsman, applicable Award | All |
| Employment contracts and onboarding | Fair Work Ombudsman | All |
| Leave, absence and termination | Fair Work Ombudsman | All |
| Superannuation | Australian Taxation Office | All |
| Work health and safety | Safe Work Australia and the state regulator | All |
| Workers compensation and injury management | The state insurer | All |
| Food safety program and allergen control | FSANZ, local council or state health department | All |
| Fire, emergency and evacuation | State regulator and the building's fire order | All |
| First aid | Safe Work Australia code of practice | All |
| Cash handling and security | Insurer requirements and the state regulator | All |
| Card payment handling | PCI DSS via the payment provider | All |
| Privacy and customer data | Office of the Australian Information Commissioner | All |
| Insurance and renewals | The venue's broker | All |
| Tax, BAS and record keeping | Australian Taxation Office | All |
| Waste, recycling and grease trap | Local council and the water authority | All |
| Liquor licensing and responsible service | State liquor authority | Bar, pub, restaurant, hotel, large format |
| Gaming | State gaming regulator | Pub, large format |

Where a venue ticks "we do not have one", the module records an open obligation, shows it on the Systems screen as outside the audit, and leaves it until the operator marks it sourced. An "add another" field catches anything missed.
